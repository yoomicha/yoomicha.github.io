Sales AI Assistant Proposal

A proposal for an AI chat interface that unifies fragmented customer data across Cisco's 30+ internal seller tools — designed to cut the time sellers spend hunting for information before they can act on it.

Overview

Cisco sellers spend nearly 20% of their time searching for accurate, holistic customer and solution information scattered across 30+ internal tools. This proposal explores an AI chat interface — backed by user, customer, solution, and enablement agents — that surfaces that information in one place, reducing the workflow by at least 7 steps.

Collective pain points by persona across the selling motion

Insights

Four core use cases emerged

  • Reviewing data-related pain points across the selling motion
  • Calibrating how much of the workflow should be human-in-the-loop vs. automated
  • Four core use cases: finding customer data, insight-based prioritization, showcasing value, automating redundancy
  • Generative research: RFP generation process, 12 moderated user interviews
  • An Art of Possible workshop with stakeholders to pressure-test the vision

Findings from the generative research: complexity varied greatly (20–400 questions) with high compliance risk, meaning data must be verified; short timelines created high risk of dead loss; and primitive workarounds already existed, varying by team.

Trust needed a researcher in the loop

Collaborating closely with UX researchers turned raw data into actionable, high-impact design strategies — and welcoming contrasting opinions from engineering, security, and product surfaced edge cases early, leading to a more balanced, compliant experience.

Learning

Designing one voice across a fragmented ecosystem

The power of researcher partnership: collaborating closely with UX researchers proved essential in transforming raw data into actionable, high-impact design strategies. This cross-functional partnership allowed for deeper empathy with users and ensured product decisions were rooted in validated insights rather than assumptions.

Embracing diverse perspectives for robust UX: synthesizing contrasting opinions from engineering, security, and product teams significantly refined the solution. Welcoming these varied viewpoints helped identify edge cases early and led to a more balanced, compliant, and inclusive experience. Moving forward, integrating these insights with the Renewals AI Support console and other customer-facing surfaces will be essential to a seamless, cohesive AI experience across the enterprise.
